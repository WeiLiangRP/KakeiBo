Where it started

Hani Motoko, 1904

Hani Motoko was Japan's first female journalist. In 1904 she introduced the kakeibo — literally "household ledger" — in a women's magazine, as a way for ordinary households to actually see their money instead of guessing at it.

The method didn't ask people to earn more or cut everything out. It asked one household to sit down at the start of each month, write down four honest answers, and check back in at the end. That was the whole system. It still is — we just moved the notebook into Telegram.

1904 Hani Motoko publishes the first kakeibo in a Japanese women's magazine.
1920s Kakeibo notebooks become a fixture in Japanese households, sold every December for the year ahead.

2026 We build the four questions into a Telegram bot — without losing the parts that made people actually use it.
The four columns

Every entry belongs to one of four honest buckets

Needs

Rent, groceries, utilities, transport — the costs of simply running a household.

Wants

Dining out, hobbies, subscriptions, the coffee you didn't need but enjoyed anyway.

Culture

Books, museums, classes — spending that grows you, kept separate so it never gets cut first.

Unexpected

Repairs, medical costs, gifts — the honest category most budgets pretend doesn't exist.
